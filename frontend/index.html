Plant bio-electric monitoring

Plants speak in millivolts.
PhytoSense listens.

Electrodes on two leaves, a 16-bit converter and a soil-moisture sensor watch a money plant's electrical signal live. A trained detector flags disturbances within seconds, careful control tests show what is really the plant, and an AI assistant explains it all in plain language.

How it works

  1. 1

    Sense

    An electrode pair captures the plant's surface bio-potential — a slow-moving electrical signal that shifts with hydration, light, and touch.

  2. 2

    Filter

    A 50 Hz notch filter strips mains interference; a moving average smooths sensor noise, leaving the genuine physiological trend.

  3. 3

    Detect & test

    A model trained on 18 labelled recordings flags sudden electrical disturbances live. Control tests (plastic pen, a second amplifier) showed those come mostly from the human body, so we now track slow drought stress with a soil-moisture reference.

  4. 4

    Explain

    An LLM turns the classification and readouts into a plain status update, and stays available to answer follow-up questions.
